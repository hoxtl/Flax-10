\section{Objectives, context and key stakeholders}
\label{sect:objectives}

Higher education demands a level of autonomous organisation that many students find genuinely difficult to sustain. At KCL, students are expected to independently track deadlines, manage coursework across multiple modules, attend society commitments, and maintain a healthy work-life balance, all without a single system to support them. The administrative burden falls equally on teaching staff, who frequently field repeated queries about module details, reading lists, and submission dates that could be resolved by a well-designed information platform.

Flax-10 identified three primary stakeholder groups whose needs shaped the objectives of StudyFlax:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{KCL Students}: the primary users, who stand to gain the most from reduced organisational burden and a clearer view of their academic and personal commitments.
    \item \textbf{KCL Teaching Staff}: who benefit from a direct channel to broadcast module information to students, reducing repetitive administrative communication.
    \item \textbf{KCL Society Organisers}: who gain a dedicated space to reach and engage their members without relying on external platforms.
\end{itemize}

These stakeholders informed three core objectives for StudyFlax. The first is \textbf{consolidation}: to give students a single platform through which all academic and extracurricular commitments are visible and actionable, eliminating the need to consult multiple disconnected tools. The second is \textbf{personalisation}: to provide productivity support that adapts to a student's individual habits and workload, rather than imposing a rigid, one-size-fits-all schedule. The third is \textbf{communication}: to close the information gap between teaching staff and students, ensuring that module content, deadlines, and announcements reach students directly within the tool they already use to manage their time.

Together, these objectives reflect a belief that the right digital infrastructure can meaningfully reduce the stress and cognitive overhead that student life imposes.

\section{Requirements and specifications}
\label{sect:requirements}

The requirements for StudyFlax were derived directly from the three objectives above. The system that was delivered can be understood as a set of feature areas, each of which addresses one or more of the objectives of consolidation, personalisation, and communication.

\textbf{Calendar and timetable integration.} Students can access the Calendar page through the main navigation. This area of the system allows users to create personal events, manage recurring events, and import timetable data from KCL's iCalendar feed. Its primary contribution is consolidation: academic timetables, personal plans, and other commitments are brought into one view rather than being split across separate systems.

\textbf{Task management.} Students can access the Tasks page from the main navigation and can also interact with task data through the homepage dashboard. The delivered system supports task lists, task completion, prioritisation, time logging, and links between tasks and related academic work. This feature area supports consolidation by giving users a dedicated place to organise actionable work, and also supports personalisation by allowing students to structure their workload in the way that suits them best.

\textbf{Focus timer and study tracking.} The timer is accessible from the main navigation and from timer-related controls in the interface. It records structured study sessions and contributes to statistics such as streaks, study history, and weekly progress. This supports personalisation by helping students build routines around their own working habits rather than simply storing deadlines passively.

\textbf{Auto-scheduler and dashboard customisation.} Students can access the auto-scheduler from the scheduling interface and can personalise the homepage through widget configuration. The auto-scheduler uses deadlines and user settings, such as sleep schedule and preferred study time, to generate a revision plan. These features were specified to ensure the platform does not merely display information, but actively adapts to the student's context and preferences.

\textbf{Modules and academic information.} Teaching staff can access the Modules section to create and manage modules, deadlines, and reading lists, while students can view the same information in the student-facing interface. This feature area supports communication by providing a direct route through which academic information is published and consumed inside the same platform students use to organise their time.

\textbf{Societies and announcements.} Students can browse and join societies through the Societies section, while society organisers can manage announcements and events through society administration interfaces. Society events can also be synced into a student's calendar. This supports both consolidation and communication: extracurricular activity is surfaced in the same environment as academic planning, and organisers gain a direct channel to their members.

\textbf{Group work.} The Group Work area allows students to manage shared projects, assign tasks, record member availability, and suggest meeting times. This feature extends the platform from individual organisation into small-scale collaboration, reflecting the reality that much student work is coordinated across groups rather than completed alone.

\textbf{Analytics, AI support, and campus map.} The Analytics page presents study history and progress information, while the AI study coach provides contextual feedback based on recorded activity. The Campus Map page supports route planning to KCL campuses using the student's saved home address. These features broaden the system beyond basic record-keeping by helping students interpret their behaviour and plan around the physical realities of attending university.

Alongside these functional requirements, the project was shaped by several non-functional expectations. Usability was a priority throughout, as a platform intended to reduce cognitive load must not itself become difficult to use. Security was also essential, because the application stores personal, academic, and role-sensitive data. Finally, the system needed to remain accessible across desktop and mobile form factors so that students could consult and update it throughout the day rather than only when seated at a computer.
